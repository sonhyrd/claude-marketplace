## Stop — do not write the `routeFromHAR` line yet

`ls` showing no file is **not yet evidence of a failed recording**, and it is also not permission to proceed. Playwright flushes `recordHar` **on context close** (`probe.mjs:429`, `:614`) — batches answering normally says nothing about the HAR, because during the batches no HAR exists by design. The file appears when the probe closes, and only then.

What must not happen: committing `page.routeFromHAR('tests/e2e/<feature>.api.har', { notFound: 'abort' })` against a file that isn't there. Under `notFound: 'abort'` every read aborts, and in Step 7 that reads as a broken application — you'd spend the whole three-attempt heal budget on a missing recording. `probe.mjs` says this in its own warning text: *"Do NOT commit a routeFromHAR spec against a HAR that does not exist."*

### Diagnose in this order

**1. Close the probe and read its stderr — the likeliest answer is "not flushed yet."**

```bash
node <skill-base>/scripts/probe.mjs close
```

Three outcomes, and the line you read decides everything:

- `probe: HAR written <path> (<n> bytes, filter **/api/**) — …` → the file exists now, scrubbed at capture. Go write the network determinism. Also check for a `probe: REFUSED` line beneath it — residue survived the scrub, and that recording must not be committed.
- `probe: WARNING — RECORD_HAR was set but no HAR landed …` → recording ran, nothing matched `**/api/**`. Real cause on proxy-shaped apps (`/api/request?cmd=`) is usually a filter mismatch — compare against what the recon `network-summary` actually listed, then re-record with a corrected `HAR_URL_FILTER`.
- Nothing about a HAR at all → the daemon never had `RECORD_HAR` (case 2).

**2. If the close is silent on HAR: the daemon predated your env.** A `send` autostarts a daemon inheriting *that* command's environment, and a second `start` against a live daemon exits 1 with exactly this warning (`probe.mjs:401`). If a daemon was already up when you set `RECORD_HAR`, it never had it — the whole recon pass recorded nothing, and "the batches answered normally" is precisely how that failure looks. Fix: `close`, `start` again with `RECORD_HAR` and `BASE_URL` set, and **re-drive the recon batches** — the recording only captures traffic that happens while it's open.

**3. If the probe was backgrounded with a trailing `&` or SIGKILLed:** no flush path ran. Re-record. The raw staging capture may survive in `$TMPDIR/pwprove-har-*/capture.har` — do not copy it into the tree; it is unscrubbed and authenticated, which is the exact thing capture-time scrubbing exists to prevent.

### On resuming Step 5

Re-record before generating, not after. The alternative — writing the spec now and letting Step 7 discover it — burns a full proof run, and the failure mode is the one the failure-handling table flags as most misleading: *every* read aborting means a binding or recording problem, not a short recording.

One thing to keep straight when the HAR does land: the mutation under assertion still gets a hand-written `route.fulfill`. Only the reads come from the recording.
