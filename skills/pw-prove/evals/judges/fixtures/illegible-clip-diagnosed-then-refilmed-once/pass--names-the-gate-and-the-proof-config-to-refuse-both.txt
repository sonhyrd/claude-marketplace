First, the inspection itself — what each frame shows:

- **Clip 1** — the saved banner, centred and settled. Legible.
- **Clip 2** — the saved banner on the second record, centred and settled. Legible.
- **Clip 3** — the record list with the success toast cut off against the right edge of the frame.
  Illegible.

**I diagnose clip 3 before anything is re-filmed.** The subject is not missing and not blurred; it
sits against the frame edge, so this is element-off-frame. Re-filming without a fix is pointless
here — the run is deterministic and would reproduce the same frame.

**The fix**, in the committed spec, at the moment of the hold:

```ts
await toast.evaluate((n) => n.scrollIntoView({ block: 'center', inline: 'center' }));
// JUSTIFIED: payoff dwell — holds the asserted toast long enough to read.
if (process.env.PW_PROVE_CLIP) await page.waitForTimeout(1200);
```

Two things this is deliberately **not**:

- It is **ungated**. I do not put the scrollIntoView behind PW_PROVE_CLIP. The filming law is that
  the variable may only ever add time, and framing is not time — a gated scroll would mean CI runs a
  differently-positioned page from the film.
- It goes in the **committed spec**, not the proof config. The proof config carries recording
  settings; a framing change that only exists there is a change CI never sees.

**Then, in order:**

1. Re-run the Step-6 clip-fidelity audit on the edited spec and require **exit 0** before re-filming.
   Moving the dwell can drop its `// JUSTIFIED:` marker, and that is a Step-6 failure I would
   otherwise carry into the film.
2. `rm -rf test-results`
3. Re-film **once**, and re-extract the frames.

If the second frame is still illegible I **publish it with an explicit warning** —
`Clip 3 — illegible (element off-frame), published with warning` — rather than stopping. A bad clip
is not a failed test, and I do not re-film a second time chasing it.
