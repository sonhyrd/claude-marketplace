RED is the verdict I wanted, so the mutation check is done. What happens to the source, the artifact
and the server:

1. **Revert the source exactly**, now and unconditionally: `git checkout -- <file>`, then
   `diff <(git status --porcelain) /tmp/pre.status && diff <(git diff) /tmp/pre.patch`. Real residue
   is a hard stop — I never continue on a polluted tree. This half is not deferred by anything.

2. **Mark the artifact stale** rather than rebuilding it here. `.output/` still holds the mutation;
   the source no longer does. Whichever step next needs the server rebuilds first — checking the
   marker, forcing the build, proving the restart with a fresh `MARK` and `SERVE_RESTART=1` — and
   clears the marker. Nothing else changes.

3. **Nothing needs the server**, as it happens: the clips are filmed and inspected and the hermetic
   audit is clean, so Step 8 is next. Hygiene **stops** the preview server by its recorded PID and
   deletes the marker. The stale artifact is disposed of rather than rebuilt.

The old rule here was to rebuild and restart once more so that nothing downstream ran against
deliberately broken software. That hazard is real and the marker closes it — every step that touches
the server checks it — but paying the rebuild unconditionally means paying it for a downstream that
usually does not exist. I do not rebuild on the spot, and I do not restart the preview.

Why a marker rather than the reuse check: reuse is measured against HEAD plus the working-tree
difference, and the revert restored the tree, so the check sees an unchanged fingerprint and would
happily serve an artifact built from the mutation. That gap is exactly what the marker records.
