### Changed

- `delegate-tickets` skill: **six rules the loaded orchestration guide already states are deleted, and three false statements are made true.** Step 0 loads all 408 lines of the live guide on every run, so anything the guide carries was a second copy ageing independently of the one already in the coordinator's context. Verified against `skills get orchestration --full` and removed: ack-or-replay (the guide: *"replays that exact batch until `--ack <delivery_id>`"*); a timeout or `{count:0}` being a checkpoint; workers running 15–60 minutes; accounting for settled terminals by reuse, `worker-retain` or `worker-release`; keeping rolling until every Dispatch settles; and the dispatch-capability revocation note — the weakest of the six and recorded as such: the guide states the *consequence* (a settled worker sends no further lifecycle messages) and not the mechanism, so the mechanism is what went, while the instruction it carried — **re-tasking the worker is not on the table** — stays in step 6 in its own right. A one-line pointer into the guide was rejected as the fix — it goes stale the same way, only more quietly. Step 5's preamble justified carrying Orca's rules on the grounds that `orca-cli` and `orchestration` are outside this repo's write access, which was written before the guide was loaded; one line replaces it — *the guide is authoritative for lifecycle, this file carries only what it does not*. **Five rules stay**, four because the guide genuinely does not carry them and `--timeout-ms` on the narrower ground that the guide says *always pass it* while documenting no default — it is that gap, not the instruction, the skill exists to state: always pass `--timeout-ms`; message bodies as `"$(cat <file>)"`; verify `--base-branch` actually took; `Unable to write index` is contention, not a conflict; and GitHub's `issue_dependencies_summary.blocked_by` lags the real edges. The **shared-stash rule keeps its "never" and its reasoning**, alone among the prohibitions, because `git stash` is the *correct* reflex in a single checkout and the damage lands in a sibling worktree nobody looks at — the positive form does not explain why the obvious move is wrong. The other four become the instruction they were guarding: pass message bodies as `"$(cat <file>)"`, diff against `git merge-base`, take the tickets' blocking edges as the DAG, resolve `implement`'s path on this machine. Three statements were false as written. **"A turn ends in exactly three places"** named the final report, an escalation and step 1's gate while step 4 alone stops four more times — a closed list a reader can falsify from the same file stops being a lookup, which is the entire reason it was written closed. The list keeps its form and its third entry widens to **refusal-to-start**, of which every preflight stop is one kind. **"No receipt, no merge"** was a heading its own body contradicted: the body runs the Standards axis on a missing receipt and then merges. ADR-0010's rule is deliberate and unchanged — at the measured ~1-in-3 trip rate a real refusal would strand a third of all branches on an escalation — so the *heading* is what was wrong, and it now claims what the body does: **a branch merges reviewed, or not at all**. And briefs and receipts now share one `/tmp/<ticket-slug>/` placeholder. The concurrency cap stays at 2 on the merge-back review surface alone; the setup-hook contention half of its justification is deleted, `worker-start` having turned a half-built worker into a nonzero exit that names its own residuals. `make check-delegate-cli` still exits 0, now over 10 commands rather than 13 — the three that went were named only by the deleted rules. `tests/test_delegate_tickets.py` grows to 63 cases, asserting each deleted rule absent over every doc the plugin ships, each surviving rule present, the three turn-end categories, the receipt heading against its body, the stash rule's prohibition *and* its reasoning, the cap's remaining justification, and one `/tmp` placeholder; every ADR-0010 receipt clause is carried over unchanged in force. [ADR-0010](docs/adr/0010-delegate-tickets-gates-merge-back-on-a-review-receipt.md) and [ADR-0011](docs/adr/0011-delegate-tickets-dispatches-through-worker-start.md) each take a one-line correction rather than a silent contradiction — 0010's vocabulary paragraph carried the slogan the skill took its heading from, and 0011's reasoning cited the step-7 accounting clause deleted here. Neither decision changes

- `delegate-tickets` skill: **one supervised `worker-start` replaces the four hand-rolled calls that built a worker, and the engine argv table is retired.** Step 5 built each worker with `worktree create`, a `terminal create` carrying the run's engine argv, `terminal wait --for tui-idle`, and a five-retry `terminal read` loop that required a rendered agent frame. The bundled orchestration guide names `worker-start` the preferred supervised path, and it composes all four: `--task`, `--worktree new-child`, `--name`, `--base-branch <integration-branch>`, `--agent`, `--setup run`, plus `--model` / `--effort` where the user named them. It **exits 0 only for `ready`**, decided on process identity rather than on a rendered frame, so the readiness loop it approximated is deleted and a nonzero exit is what now means the worker did not start — read as `stage` / `effects` / `residualResources`, never guessed at and never automatically retried (`--retry-of <dispatch_id>` is the deliberate replacement, and it inherits no placement). The old path also left the worker **unsupervised**: no `worker_dispatches` row, so `worker-stop` and `worker-release` took no action and step 7's "reuse it or release it" named nothing a coordinator could do. It is reachable now, and step 7 spells both halves. `--base-branch` verification survives unchanged — an ignored flag and an absent flag still fail identically. So does the `cursor` Workspace Trust gate, restated against `worker-start` and sharper for it: readiness inspects the process table, and a `cursor-agent` parked at its trust box *is* the foreground process and *is* a recognised agent, so `ready` comes back for a worker that will never move. `references/worker-engines.md` becomes `references/worker-launch.md`, rewritten around `--agent` / `--model` / `--effort` (`--effort` requires `--model`, and neither combines with `--terminal` — which step 7's terminal reuse is exactly where it trips); the argv table's one load-bearing column was the permissionless flag, and Orca supplies that from app config keyed by engine id. `references/orchestration-probe.md` is merged into step 4 as a preflight-failures section and deleted, its `run_required` and bare-usage-error cases dying with the probe task #72 retired, leaving exactly two references. The low-level custom-argv path stays documented as the escape hatch the guide itself names, not as the default. [ADR-0011](docs/adr/0011-delegate-tickets-dispatches-through-worker-start.md) records the trade; ADR-0006 gets a one-line correction on the `implement` path it cited, and is not superseded.
- `delegate-tickets` skill: **the preflight is one ordered sequence ending in the run's own `run-create`, and it no longer leaves a `PROBE` task behind.** Step 4 used to prove the lifecycle writes by creating a throwaway task and retiring it with `task-update --status completed`, because the CLI has no delete verb — so every run permanently added a row. Worse, it created that task *before* any Run was bound, so on a clean machine its likeliest answer was `run_required`: a setup step read as a failure. The run has to create a Run anyway, so that `run-create` is the fence probe now — the same evidence, no artifact, and still ahead of every worktree, which was the whole argument for doing it first. Two preconditions the skill omitted join the sequence: `status --json` must show a reachable runtime (help text is local, every `orchestration` verb is an RPC), and **the host's agent permission mode must be yolo**, read from `settings.agentDefaultArgs` in the active Orca profile. That one is app config, not an argument the skill can pass, and a host left on manual launches workers that stop at an approval prompt nobody is watching — indistinguishable from a slow worker until the dispatch times out. Orca's experimental orchestration toggle is named as the cause of *absent* lifecycle verbs, which read exactly like a fenced runtime and are not one. `references/orchestration-probe.md` now separates all four failures — fenced runtime, stopped runtime, disabled feature, manual-mode host — each with one next step. The unverifiable claim that "`run-create` succeeding proves nothing — the runtime can be fenced per-command" is dropped: the probe reference's own description says the *whole lifecycle* is fenced, and no per-command fence could be reproduced.

- `CONTEXT.md`: **`Dispatch` is now Orca's noun, and `Open dispatch` becomes `Unsettled dispatch`.** The glossary had two meanings of *dispatch* a line apart: its own **Open dispatch**, and Orca's first-class **Dispatch** — one Task attempt assigned to a terminal, with its own id and lifecycle — which `delegate-tickets` is being rewritten onto, where every lifecycle message carries a `--dispatch-id`. **Dispatch** is now defined as Orca defines it, and the coordinator-knowledge state is renamed with the word the orchestration guide already uses for a Dispatch that has reported: it *settles*. The distinction the old entry drew is untouched. Two entries join them: **Receipt**, which `delegate-tickets` already leans on twice without defining, and **Supervised worker**, which separates a worker Orca started as a Dispatch of its own from a terminal an operator started and was handed a dispatch afterwards — Orca reports the second as `unsupervised` and never owns its cleanup. Every existing entry is unchanged, and `CONTEXT.md` stays a glossary and nothing else.

- `delegate-tickets` skill: **the coordinator now resolves its Orca binary and loads the live orchestration guide before anything mutates.** On Linux, bare `orca` is the Electron launcher: it prints a single-instance notice and **exits 0** for every argument list, so a coordinator running `orca orchestration task-create` read a clean exit code for a run in which nothing was orchestrated — and every command in the skill was spelled that way, under an invariant claiming "step 4's probe resolves it once for every command below" when step 4 held no resolution logic at all. A new **step 0** runs above every profile read, worktree, terminal and lifecycle call. It orders the candidates by where it is running (`orca-ide` outside an Orca-managed pane, `orca` inside) and then **proves** one by requiring it to answer `orchestration --help` — preference orders, evidence selects, because exit 0 is exactly what the launcher gives and cannot be the test. Every command in `SKILL.md` and `references/` is now written `<orca> …` against that resolved name. Step 0 then loads `<orca> skills get orchestration --full` — 408 lines, bundled in the binary and version-matched to it — and requires it read before the first lifecycle call, so lifecycle facts come from the running runtime rather than from prose in this repo that ages against it. It is **loaded, never vendored**: a snapshot under `~/.claude/skills/` disagrees with the runtime silently. `/orchestration` is dropped as a skill dependency, having never been a marketplace plugin nor installed on any machine here; its five deferrals become pointers to the loaded guide. `references/quickstart.md` is **deleted** — authored as a docs-site page inside the vendored `matt` tree (`eb1c7e2`) and moved into `plugins/sss/` as a pure rename (`a743758`), it told readers to install this skill with `npx skills add mattpocock/skills` from a repo that has never contained it, and its Prerequisites section omitted the two things `SKILL.md` pointed at it *for*. Its whitespace-flattening helper is why a clause straddling a line break or a blockquote marker no longer decides whether the clause is present.

- `delegate-tickets` skill: **a coordinator's turn now ends in exactly three places, and every other stop is a bug.** Measured on run `run_97a82d3add36`: the coordinator left `check --wait` to do local work while #1063 was still open — a byte-budget measurement, a filed issue, a profile amend, all of them things step 6 asks for — finished it, and ended the turn on a recap that *named* the next merge and the next dispatch. The old step 7 was two sentences — "run to completion", "coordinate through `/orchestration`" — which is an exhortation, and a model holding a tidy recap will read a tidy recap as done. The rule leads with the positive — **a coordinator's resting state is `check --wait`** — because a prohibition drags the forbidden behaviour into context and makes it more available, not less; naming the state the coordinator returns to excludes idling without spending a line on it. The handoff's other suggested rule — *don't start local work that cannot finish inside the current wait window* — is **deliberately not taken**: it is unenforceable, since no model can predict whether a measurement fits in fifteen minutes, and it is not the failure, since overrunning the window costs nothing if the wait is re-entered afterwards.
